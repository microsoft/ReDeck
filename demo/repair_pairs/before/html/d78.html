Cross-market synthesis

Across eight utility solicitations and three merchant markets, onshore wind remained the lowest-cost bulk MWh source, while solar paired with storage delivered the strongest evening reliability value and the fastest construction timeline.

Primary findings

  • Solar-plus-storage closed the dispatchability gap

    In summer peak windows, paired assets captured 78% of the capacity credit assigned to gas peakers, up from 51% in the 2021 benchmark set.

  • Onshore wind still leads on unsubsidized bulk energy cost

    Median levelized cost landed at $34/MWh in high-resource regions, but interconnection delays widened project risk premiums by 180 basis points.

  • Offshore wind improved output quality, not headline price

    Capacity factors above 49% materially reduced winter supply volatility, yet capex remained more than double utility-scale solar in the sampled pipeline.

  • Geothermal remained niche but operationally valuable

    Where resource quality was proven, geothermal delivered the highest availability and the lowest balancing requirement of any zero-carbon technology reviewed.

Storage duration—not panel cost—is now the decisive procurement variable
Implication for planners
Procurement teams should segment renewable tenders by delivery profile rather than technology class, because evening ramp support, curtailment exposure, and interconnection timing now explain more variance in realized project value than nominal PPA price alone.
