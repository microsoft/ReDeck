Top findings

    Recall improved most on low-volume overnight shifts

    The model lifted urgent-case recall from 81.9% to 86.6% between 11pm and 6am, when attending coverage was thinnest and queue latency was highest.

    Median time-to-first-review fell despite stable reading volume

    Priority routing reduced median first-review time from 22 minutes to 14 minutes for positive studies, with no measurable slowdown for routine chest CTs.

    Calibration remained consistent across scanner vendors

    Performance varied by less than 1.2 AUC points across Siemens, GE, and Philips cohorts, suggesting limited site-specific tuning was required before deployment.

    0.91macro AUC
  • 04

    False positives concentrated in post-surgical cases

    Most erroneous escalations were linked to clips, drains, and motion artifacts; adding 4,200 post-op studies to retraining cut this subgroup error by 18%.

    18%error reduction
Operational implication
Sites with overnight backlog and limited subspecialty coverage saw the largest benefit. The study recommends deploying AI triage as a queue-ordering layer rather than an autonomous diagnostic gate, with targeted retraining on post-surgical imaging before broader rollout.
